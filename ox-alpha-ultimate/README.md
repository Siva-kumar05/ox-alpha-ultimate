# ox-alpha

`ox-alpha` is a long-only intraday agent for NSE equities. Once it has completed secure boot, it autonomously chooses validated strategy parameters, buys, attaches a broker-managed target and stop, sells on either exit, sells on an opposite signal, and squares off at the configured time. It never needs a per-trade approval.

It is safe by default: `config.yaml` starts in local paper mode and no credential, token, or broker account number is stored in the project.

## What changed in the secure build

- No fabricated order fills, prices, or news. A failed/unconfirmed broker call stops trading rather than appearing successful.
- Every live entry is a Dhan **Super Order**, so the entry, target, and stop-loss reach the broker as one order. The local database changes only after a confirmed fill.
- Live brackets use Wilder ATR and the `sl_atr`/`tp_atr` values of the approved strategy or score-weighted approved strategies that actually fired; no generic live stop/target rule replaces the validated one.
- A negative signal automatically sells an agent-owned long. Naked short orders are deliberately unsupported.
- Strategy rows are parameter-only records for three audited templates. Database-stored Python is quarantined and never executed.
- Strategy validation is execution-aligned: it is long-only, uses causally confirmed swing structure, executes historical signals at the next candle open, models configured slippage and Indian transaction charges, and can promote only from embargoed expanding walk-forward results across several symbols. Strategies generated by older validation rules are quarantined and retrained.
- The active set is a small score-weighted ensemble of constrained, readable templates. The newest template requires trend, session VWAP, relative volume, volatility, and a prior-high breakout to agree. Scores are capped; undefined downside-risk statistics, unprofitable tests, weak profit factors, unstable signals, and insufficient out-of-sample trades cannot be promoted.
- New entries stop at `entry_cutoff` (14:45 IST by default); protective exits, opposite-signal exits, and the configured square-off still operate. Weekends and the dates in `market_holidays` are blocked.
- Critical live actions are appended to an HMAC-linked audit chain. Live boot refuses to run without an audit secret or if the existing chain does not verify.
- Order IDs are persisted with positions; restart reconciliation fails closed on a broker/database mismatch or an unexpected account position.
- Order flow is the primary entry gate. In live mode the agent consumes Dhan's documented 20-level Full Market Depth feed, pairs only contemporaneous bid and ask ladders, and blocks every new long when the book is stale, thin, wide, or not buy-side supportive. The candle strategy is a secondary regime confirmation; the final risk and order-management gates still run after it.
- A primary live order-flow gate also requires a retained real `DHAN_DEPTH20` replay study to meet its configured sample, hit-rate, and mean-forward-return criteria. This is a gate-validation study, not an execution or profitability backtest: it does not model fills, fees, or queue priority.
- Rate-limited read-only market-data calls back off exponentially within a bounded delay. An uncertain order mutation remains fail-closed and is reconciled rather than blindly retried.
- The dashboard records the derived depth metrics: best bid/ask, displayed-book imbalance, displayed-book change, smoothed persistent pressure, support-snapshot count, depth-and-spread liquidity quality, microprice edge, freshness, and entry/exit gate. It does not pretend candle volume, displayed liquidity, or a feed without aggressor flags is executed buyer/seller delta or queue position. Paper depth is visibly labelled simulated and is never accepted in live mode.
- The kill switch only acts on positions recorded as agent-owned. Use a dedicated Dhan account—mixing manual positions with this agent makes reconciliation halt rather than risking someone else's position.
- The smoke test runs in a temporary directory and never deletes `oxalpha.db`, logs, or a live account's state.

## Dashboard

Double-click [start-dashboard.cmd](start-dashboard.cmd) to open the local browser dashboard at `http://127.0.0.1:8501`. It refreshes positions, trades, strategy validation, equity history, and audit events every five seconds. It also exposes an emergency stop button which creates `KILL.flag`; the running agent then flattens its recorded positions on the next check.

The dashboard is deliberately bound to `127.0.0.1`, so it is not exposed to your Wi-Fi or the internet and never displays Dhan credentials. To use it from a phone, connect to the laptop with a remote-desktop tool; do not expose the dashboard port directly to a network.

It now has a TradingView-style price chart with candles, recorded volume, SMA 20/50 and session VWAP; a watchlist; a relative-performance chart; a volume/relative-strength scanner; and a strategy/audit page. The chart ranges are in market order: **1D, 5D, 1M, 3M, and All available**. Longer ranges are automatically aggregated (5-minute, 15-minute, hourly, then daily) so the candles remain readable. If there is not enough stored history, the page says so and shows only what exists.

The Scanner page also has the **Order-flow admission monitor**, and **Flow intelligence** shows a live pressure timeline, the most recent 20-level bid/ask ladder, liquidity quality, and the exact admission decision. The initial `min_observations: 300` is an in-memory warm-up, not historical proof of a profitable order-flow strategy; retain and evaluate representative recorded depth before using live capital.

`history_days: 95` tells the agent to retain enough one-minute history for the three-month view and training. Dhan’s v2 intraday historical endpoint supports date ranges and minute candles; 95 days is a deliberately bounded local default, not a claim that every range is available before the first sync. The paper broker labels its generated history as simulated; it must never be treated as a market-data result.

## Autonomous live setup

Complete these one-time prerequisites before switching modes:

1. Use a dedicated Dhan intraday account for this agent, with no manual positions or working orders.
2. Register the server's actual static egress IP in Dhan and set that same address only in the host environment, not in `config.yaml`:

```powershell
$env:DHAN_STATIC_IP = "your-server-static-ip"
```

The agent checks both its actual egress address and Dhan's reported registered address at boot.
3. Ensure the configured `security_map` is current for every listed symbol, using Dhan's official instrument master.
   The included depth adapter subscribes to Dhan's 20-level Full Market Depth feed for the configured NSE-equity IDs. If that subscription cannot connect or becomes stale, primary order-flow mode fails closed for new entries.
4. Provide secrets through the host's secret manager or environment—never in `config.yaml` or source control:

```powershell
$env:DHAN_CLIENT_ID = "your-client-id"
$env:DHAN_PIN = "your-six-digit-dhan-pin"
$env:DHAN_TOTP_SECRET = "your-totp-secret"
$env:OX_AUDIT_KEY = "a-long-random-secret-of-at-least-32-characters"
```

**Recommended (daily token, no secrets at rest):** do NOT store `DHAN_PIN`/`DHAN_TOTP_SECRET` on this machine. Each morning, generate a 24-hour access token in the dhan.co web console (Profile -> DhanHQ Trading APIs -> Generate Access Token; the PIN/TOTP prompt happens in the browser, so the 2FA seed never touches this PC), then launch:

```powershell
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File START_DAILY.ps1   # or double-click start-daily.cmd
```

The script masks the paste, validates the token against the read-only `/fundlimit` endpoint before booting, keeps it in process-scope environment only, and scrubs it when the agent exits. `DHAN_TOKEN` alone is sufficient for the agent's login path; `DHAN_PIN`/`DHAN_TOTP_SECRET` are only needed if you later want unattended automatic renewal.

`OX_AUDIT_KEY` is required in live mode and must remain only in the host's secret manager/environment; changing it makes the historic audit chain unverifiable and correctly blocks live boot.

5. Add the official NSE holiday dates for the current calendar year to `market_holidays`. Live mode refuses to start without them. Keep `tick_seconds` at 2 seconds or more; the shipped 3-second value leaves rate-limit headroom.

6. Retain enough genuine Dhan depth and matching candle data for the configured primary-gate replay thresholds. Live primary order-flow mode remains halted until that evidence is present.

7. Change only these two lines in `config.yaml`:

```yaml
mode: live
platform: dhan
```

Then start it:

```powershell
python run.py run
```

The agent will deploy capital automatically inside the configured risk limits after that setup. It fails closed if the static IP, credentials, broker position state, data feed, or order confirmation is not valid. A `KILL.flag` means it halted deliberately; investigate it before removing the file and restarting.

## Regulatory and broker references

Dhan documents static-IP requirements for order APIs and automatic access-token generation with TOTP in its [authentication guide](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/authentication/). Its [Super Order API](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/super-order/) documents the bundled entry, target, and stop-loss model used by this agent, and its [Full Market Depth guide](https://dhanhq.co/docs/v2/full-market-depth/) documents the 20-level binary feed used for the primary order-flow gate. India’s retail-algo framework applies to all stockbrokers from 1 April 2026 according to [SEBI’s implementation circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/sep-2025/1759232056254.pdf); ensure your broker’s current onboarding and hosting requirements are met before live use.

This software is execution infrastructure, not investment advice or a guarantee of returns. Paper-test it with representative historical and market conditions before any live deployment.

## NautilusTrader comparison and design choices

NautilusTrader has two practices that materially improve this design: process the market-book event before a strategy decision, then send any order through a final risk/execution gate; and do not validate L2/L3 ideas from OHLCV candles alone. ox-alpha now follows that sequence: fresh 20-level Dhan depth -> order-flow gate -> candle regime confirmation -> risk gate -> Dhan Super Order. It also retains the derived depth snapshots needed for later replay analysis.

The social-media examples add a useful principle but not an executable recipe for this broker: an edge must come from current, observable market state, not a prediction story. The agent therefore requires persistent book support over multiple L2 updates and enough displayed liquidity at an acceptable spread before considering an entry. It reports the evidence that led to every admission or block. See [ADR-002](docs/ADR-002-order-book-primary-admission.md) for the boundary and trade-offs.

It intentionally does **not** import NautilusTrader as its runtime. NautilusTrader is a much broader Rust-native event engine with adapters and operational requirements beyond this small Dhan/NSE application. A direct dependency would not make the Dhan adapter or a trading edge more reliable. The current boundary is smaller: Dhan-specific NSE equities, long-only intraday exposure, broker-side brackets, static-IP verification, fail-closed reconciliation, and no fund-transfer surface.

Freqtrade and Hummingbot are mature multi-exchange crypto platforms; VectorBT and Backtrader offer broader research/backtesting capabilities; TradingAgents, FinRL, and Lumibot focus on LLM/RL research workflows; CCXT is an exchange-abstraction library; and Polymarket is a separate prediction-market venue. The social-media claims about crypto or prediction-market arbitrage do not translate into a Dhan/NSE equity agent: they require real simultaneous venue access, executable quotes, fees, latency measurements, and their own risk controls. This project does not fabricate that capability.
